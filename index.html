About Me

My origin, motivation, and vision for the future of technology.

My fascination with technology started with a childhood memory: watching my father work on a computer. Out of pure curiosity and innocence, I asked him what he was doing. He smiled, turned to me, and explained that he was working with a new tool designed to revolutionize the world. From that moment on, he taught me the little he knew, leaving me completely fascinated and eager to discover how much more technology could achieve.

However, my true turning point and deep love for computer science and computing came when I joined my scholarship program and faced my very first hackathon project. Working intensely through that challenge opened my eyes: I realized that technology, artificial intelligence, and modern tools are not just abstract concepts—they are tangible forces capable of making a real, meaningful impact on people's lives and reshaping the world.

Today, driven by that mindset, my goal is to pursue Industrial Engineering in a world increasingly driven by technology. I want to merge industrial automation, process optimization, and smart systems to design efficient ecosystems where cutting-edge technology transforms real-world challenges into high-performance solutions.

EcoScan

Teachable Machine | TensorFlow.js | HTML, CSS & JavaScript
EcoScan Website
Open live website ↗

My Role: Model training. My main role was implementing and training the EcoScan classification model in Teachable Machine, and preparing it so it was ready for testing.

Problem: Many people are unsure whether an object can be recycled, which leads to recyclable materials being thrown away and non-recyclable ones contaminating the recycling bin.

Process: Trained an image classification model with Google Teachable Machine on six classes (cardboard, plastic, glass, paper, metal, and non-recyclable). Built a web interface that accesses the webcam, runs the model directly in the browser with TensorFlow.js, and applies an 80% confidence threshold so the system does not give an answer when it is unsure.

Solution: A web app that analyzes objects in real time through the camera, shows the detected material with its confidence level and a probability bar per class, and offers a recommendation on what to do with it.

Result: A working AI-powered tool that helps users make more responsible recycling decisions, demonstrating applied machine learning and interface design.
